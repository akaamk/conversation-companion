Conversation Companion

A family member answers eight questions, and the answers become a voice companion tailored to their elderly loved one.

Status: approved
What got more specific: scope said “a short questionnaire” and “one voice conversation.” Now we know it's 8 named questions on one page, a big Start button, a glowing light for listening and speaking, and two ways a conversation ends.

Home

“conversationcompanion” · A more personal kind of connection · Every story deserves a listener.
“Imagine a thoughtful conversation partner who knows what makes your loved one light up—ready to talk about their favorite subjects, listen to their stories, and keep curiosity alive.”

  • Shows the brand, headline, subhead, paragraph and a Get started button
  • Get started opens the questionnaire

Family questionnaire

  1. What name do they prefer to be called?
  2. About how old are they, and where did they grow up?
  3. How would you describe their personality?
  4. What subjects do they most enjoy talking about?
  5. What work and life experiences are they proud of, and what stories do they love to tell?
  6. Which of those should the companion know about but wait for them to bring up first?
  7. Do they have difficulty hearing or following conversation, and what helps?
  8. Are there subjects that upset them, and how should the companion respond?
  • All eight on one page, each with a free-text box
  • Only the preferred name is required (assumption)
  • Submitting moves to the Conversation screen

Conversation

Titled “Conversation Companion.” A big Start button, then a glowing light the elder can watch. Nothing to read.

listening
speaking
  • Start begins it; the elder speaks first (“Hi!” / “Let's chat”)
  • Companion replies aloud, at a measured pace
  • The light visibly differs between listening and speaking
  • The elder can tell long stories without being cut off
  • “Bye” → warm goodbye and stop
  • Silence → “Are you still there?” → no answer → goodbye, back to Start

How the companion behaves

  • Calls the elder by their preferred name
  • Brings up their favorite subjects when the conversation slows
  • Never mentions the “wait for them” experiences until the elder raises them, then responds naturally
  • Short, clear sentences; at most one question at a time; rephrases rather than repeats
  • Avoids upsetting subjects; responds calmly as the family described
  • Warm, curious, treats the elder as an adult; no memory quizzes; never pretends to be human

Look and feel

Warm, soft colors · large, easy-to-read, high-contrast text · calm and friendly · on the conversation screen, the moving light is the main visual.

What-ifs

State Didn't catch that
Calmly asks them to say it again, in simple words, without pointing out hearing or memory problems.
State Microphone blocked
Shows a clear message for the family member on how to allow it.
State Companion can't respond
A calm message for the family member; the light stops, so the elder isn't left waiting in silence.
State Elder seems distressed
Speaks calmly and suggests reaching out to a nearby caregiver or family member.
State Coming back later
Nothing is saved for the demo; a new visit starts from Home.

Building now vs. later

Building Proof of concept
  • Home page with the welcome text you wrote
  • One-page, 8-question questionnaire
  • Tailored companion from the answers plus your core rules
  • Big Start button and an animated light
  • Spoken back-and-forth conversation
  • Ending on goodbye or after silence
  • The what-if states above
Deferred Not now, with reasons
  • Voice-only start: needs an always-listening microphone
  • Saved profiles: needs storage or accounts
  • Family summary of the companion: nice for trust, not needed for the proof
  • Full 50 questions: too long for the proof of concept
Non-goals Never in this project
  • Not a caregiver, therapist or medical tool
  • No memory between conversations
  • No accounts or public launch
  • No real identifying details about your dad
  • No memory quizzes

Open questions

  • How long a silence before the check-in? Assumed 30–60 seconds, tuned during the build.
  • Which questions are required? Assumed only the name.
  • Speech recognition for older voices: a risk for the spec stage.
