Conversation Companion

A family answers nine questions about their loved one, and the answers become a voice companion tailored to that person, one that knows their stories but lets them tell them.

The main pieces

Three web pages

index.html (Home), questionnaire.html (the nine questions), conversation.html (Start button and glowing orb).

The page's behavior

js/questionnaire.js saves the answers; js/conversation.js listens, decides when a turn is over, speaks replies, and ends conversations; js/voices.js picks the voice.

The helper on Vercel

api/chat.js holds the secret key and asks Claude Haiku 5.5 for a reply; api/companion-instructions.js turns the answers into the companion's instructions.

One action, followed through the code

A family writes a story in question 6, and the companion knows it but waits to be told.

The route through the code

  1. The answer is saved

    js/questionnaire.js · search for form.addEventListener("submit" and sessionStorage.setItem

    When the family presses Create their companion, every answer, including question 6 (holdBack), is saved in the browser for this visit under the name companionAnswers. Nothing leaves the computer yet.

  2. The answers go along with every turn

    js/conversation.js · search for function loadAnswers and JSON.stringify({ answers, history })

    The Conversation page reads the saved answers when it opens. Each time the elder finishes speaking, it sends the answers and the conversation so far to the helper at /api/chat.

  3. The answers become the companion's rules

    api/companion-instructions.js · search for export function buildInstructions and HOLD_BACK_RULE; used in api/chat.js at system: buildInstructions(answers)

    Your original GPT rules come first, then an "About the person" section from the answers. Question 6 gets its own section, starting "LET THEM INTRODUCE THESE STORIES" and listing "Know but do not mention first", so Claude has the background but is told to wait for the elder to bring it up.

To change this, start here

  • How the companion behaves (tone, topics, rules): api/companion-instructions.js, the CORE_RULES text.
  • The questions: questionnaire.html for the wording; add any new field to FIELDS in js/questionnaire.js and to ANSWER_FIELDS in api/chat.js.
  • The pause before it answers: END_OF_TURN_MS (1.5 seconds), STORY_PAUSE_MS and UNFINISHED_ENDINGS near the top of js/conversation.js.
  • The Stop button: stopButton in js/conversation.js (it calls endConversation); its look is .stop-button in css/styles.css.
  • The silence check-in: IDLE_CHECK_MS, IDLE_END_MS and FINAL_GOODBYE in js/conversation.js.
  • The voices: PREFERRED_VOICES in js/voices.js (Guy first for a man's voice); the silent warm-up on Start is warmUpVoice and short openings are joined using SHORT_OPENING_WORDS, both in js/conversation.js.
  • Saying (or not saying) the name: the rule in buildInstructions in api/companion-instructions.js, and the safety net withoutTackedOnName in js/conversation.js.
  • The look: css/styles.css; the orb colors are under "The glowing orb".
  • The AI model: MODEL in api/chat.js (claude-haiku-5-5; claude-opus-5-5 is the one-line switch).

A practice to reuse

Measure before you fix. When replies felt slow, the conversation page got a testing line (?debug) that split the wait into the pause, Claude's first words, and the voice starting. Your numbers (about 2.0s + 0.8–1.5s + 0.3–1.3s) showed the pause was the biggest piece, so the decision about the pause was yours to make, and it went from 2.5s to 2s to 1.5s. The same testing line later showed Edge reporting a confidence of 1.0 even for gibberish, which ruled out one fix and pointed to another.

On another project: when something feels wrong, ask the agent to add a small, temporary readout of what's actually happening before changing anything. Then decide from the evidence rather than a guess. (See showTimings in js/conversation.js and the Revisions in devpost/checklist.md.)
