Scope · Build With AI: Basics

The Conversation Companion

A family answers a short questionnaire about their elderly loved one, and it becomes a voice companion tailored to that person.

Status: approved

How it fits together

The unique kernel

Family questionnaire → tailored companion → voice conversation.

The family's answers shape how the companion talks, what it talks about, what it avoids, and what it knows but holds back, so the elder stays the one who introduces their own stories. It's a conversation partner, never a caregiver, therapist, or memory test.

Who it's for

The elder

Someone like a 98-year-old retired NYC police officer at home with aides. Hard of hearing, increasingly isolated, loves WWII and NYC history, police work, and classic movies. Shouldn't have to type or read.

The family member

An adult child who wants their parent to have engaging company. Today the alternatives are aides who may not talk much, relatives who rarely check in, or nothing.

Why this matters

“He has no one to talk to which is isolating for him and has clearly increased signs of dementia.” It may be too late for him, so the aim is “providing it to others in similar situations.”

What “working” looks like

  1. Setup overview: a family member fills out the short questionnaire for a demo elder, based on the learner's dad with identifying details changed.
  2. Conversation: that companion has a simple spoken conversation with the elder.

The “oh, that works” moment: the companion responds to a story naturally, in short clear spoken sentences, without revealing background it was told to hold back. The family “should see that the elderly parent is being engaged… and the elderly person should be enjoying a conversation.”

What's in, what waits

Now Proof of concept
  • A short questionnaire: a handful of the most important of the 50 questions
  • Answers turn into companion instructions modeled on the original GPT instructions
  • One voice conversation: the elder speaks and hears
  • One fictionalized demo elder profile
Later Worth doing, not now
  • The full 50-question questionnaire
  • Saved profiles for multiple families; family accounts
  • The elder adding or correcting information over time
  • Family feedback and “is it making a difference” questions
  • Smart speaker or dedicated device; multiple languages and voices
  • The AI movie-creation app (separate idea)
Cut With reasons
  • Accounts and a real launch: not needed to prove the idea, and adds privacy and security work
  • Long-term memory across conversations: the questionnaire supplies background, and persistent memory raises privacy questions
  • Real identifying details about the learner's dad: the demo is public

Open risk

Voice is central. Speech recognition can struggle with older or soft voices. The spec stage chooses the voice approach and whether the demo needs a fallback.
